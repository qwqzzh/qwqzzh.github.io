Reconstruction pipeline

One plate.
Three exposures.
One color image.

Each scan stores blue, green, and red filtered exposures from top to bottom. I split the plate into equal thirds, use blue as the fixed reference, translate green and red, and stack the aligned channels in RGB order. Blue is not mandated by the assignment; it is simply a consistent coordinate system, and any channel could serve as the reference.

02

Single-scale alignment

Start small,
search everything.

For the three low-resolution JPEGs, I exhaustively evaluate every integer displacement in a [−15, 15] window. The score uses a fixed-size interior window valid for every candidate, so shifts are compared on exactly the same pixels and scanner borders do not dominate.

NCC(A,B) = ((A−μA) · (B−μB)) / (‖A−μA‖₂ ‖B−μB‖₂)

03

Multi-scale pyramid

COARSE · SEARCH ±15
×2 · REFINE ±2
×2 · REFINE ±2
×2 · REFINE ±2
FULL RESOLUTION

Large motion,
without a large search.

Full-resolution TIFFs make a wide exhaustive search too expensive. I repeatedly apply a 2×2 box filter and downsample by two until the longest side is at most 400 pixels. The coarsest level searches broadly; each finer level doubles the estimate and refines it in a ±2 neighborhood.

  • Coarsest search±15 pixels
  • Refinement±2 pixels per level
  • Scoring areafixed valid overlap, inner 80%
  • Runtimeall final images under 25 seconds
04

Required base metrics

NCC and true
Euclidean L2.

I implemented both normalized cross-correlation and the exact Euclidean distance ‖A−B‖₂ on raw RGB-channel intensities. The L2 score is negated because the search routine maximizes scores. All displacement candidates use the same number of pixels; this avoids a small-overlap candidate appearing artificially better.

L2 score(A,B) = −√Σᵢ(Aᵢ−Bᵢ)²

05

Improvement one · better features

When intensity lies,
follow the edges.

Raw channels can represent the same material with very different brightness. My first extension forced gradient features everywhere: it fixed Emir, but slightly worsened already-stable images such as Three Generations. The final rule instead computes raw NCC and L2 first. If their offsets differ by more than two pixels, only that channel is realigned with gradient-magnitude NCC. Church and Emir’s red channels trigger this fixed, filename-independent fallback.

DECISION RULE

NCC/L2 agree within 2 px → retain raw pixels. Otherwise → edge-magnitude NCC for that channel only.

06

Improvement two · automatic borders

Detect the frame,
don’t guess a margin.

The initial version left black, white, and colored plate borders. A fixed crop would hide the symptom but remove a different amount of real content from every scan. The improved detector measures line-wise luminance, channel disagreement, and clipped-pixel density near all four edges. It crops after the last statistically unusual transition in the outer 10%, followed by a small proportional guard for antialiased fringes. All thresholds are shared across the dataset.

Final images use adaptive NCC/edge alignment and automatic border removal. Offsets are (dy, dx) for green and red relative to blue; positive dy moves down and positive dx moves right.

09

What translation cannot fix

A sharp scene can still contain color ghosts.

In Three Generations and Harvesters, buildings, rails, and ground align sharply while small colored fringes remain around people. The exposures were captured sequentially, so a moving subject does not occupy the same position in every channel. One global integer translation cannot correct this local motion without damaging the static background.

LIMITATION

Global translation models camera or plate shift—not independent object motion.
